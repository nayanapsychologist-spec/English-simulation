Chapter 11 · News Report (300–350 words)

Reporting Danger

  • Structure: the inverted pyramid — lead with Who/What/When/Where/Why/How, then details, then background.
  • Vocabulary: emotive language (stunning turnaround, bleak position) and journalistic collocations (culminating in, as dusk fell).
  • Grammar: passive voice across tenses; pronoun reference for cohesion; blending direct and reported speech.
Chapter 12 · Play Script (300–350 words)

Dramatic Relationships

  • Structure: characterisation through speech, action and gesture; building dramatic irony.
  • Vocabulary: delexical verbs (take care, give permission) and speech-sound interjections (Hmmm, Ugh, Phew).
  • Grammar: the full range of question types for dialogue; imperatives; italicised stage directions.
Chronicle News · Front Page

Coastal Communities Devastated as Cyclone Vardon Makes Landfall

Stunning turnaround for emergency crews as structural defenses are breached by unprecedented storm surges.

By Linda Kerr, Chronicle News

A catastrophic category-four storm, Cyclone Vardon, has battered the northeastern coastline, forcing thousands of residents to flee their homes within designated evacuation zones. The core structural defenses of the historic port town were completely breached early Tuesday morning as torrential rain caused local rivers to burst their banks. Major arterial motorways have been completely blocked by rising floodwaters and toppled trees, severely disrupting critical rescue operations.

Initially, it was feared that hundreds of vulnerable families would be left stranded overnight. However, in a daring mission coordinated by international disaster recovery teams, over four hundred individuals have been successfully rescued by emergency helicopters and military watercraft. The displaced citizens were immediately transported to temporary emergency shelters, where medical care and supplies are being continuously replenished by humanitarian organizations.

Local administrative officials have defended their refusal to release an immediate count of missing persons, stating that they are doing so out of respect for anxious families. Meanwhile, messages of profound solidarity are being mirrored across neighboring nations, with emergency funding pouring into the affected province. Although meteorologists warn that catastrophic water restrictions will be implemented due to damaged infrastructure, relief workers remain optimistic. A senior rescue coordinator observed that while the immediate physical landscape has been utterly ruined, the collective spirit of the community has not been broken.

Task A · Core Facts & Structure
1.Identify the core newsworthy event, including the who, what, when, and where from the first paragraph.
Cyclone Vardon (what), a category-four storm, has battered the northeastern coastline (where) early Tuesday morning (when), forcing residents (who) to evacuate.
2.Why does the rescue-count detail appear in paragraph two rather than three, under the inverted pyramid structure?
The inverted pyramid places the most urgent, newsworthy update — how many people were saved — immediately after the lead, while less time-sensitive material (quotes, general background) is pushed further down.
3.What justification did officials give for withholding the count of missing persons?
They said it was out of respect for anxious families who are still waiting for confirmation.
Task B · Vocabulary & Collocations
4.Find an emotive noun phrase in Paragraph 1 that conveys the scale of the disaster.
e.g. "catastrophic category-four storm" or "unprecedented storm surges."
5.Match: battered · evacuation zones · burst their banks · continuously replenished to their meanings.
battered = hit violently and repeatedly; evacuation zones = designated areas residents must leave for safety; burst their banks = overflowed beyond a river's edges; continuously replenished = constantly restocked.
6.Explain what "stunning turnaround" means in the sub-headline.
An unexpected, dramatic reversal — from a hopeless, dangerous situation to a successful rescue outcome.
Task C · Grammar & Use of English
7.Identify the passive tense in "were completely breached" and explain its effect.
Past Simple Passive. It foregrounds the defenses (the affected thing) rather than the storm, which suits news style — the impact matters more than naming the agent.
8.Rewrite into Present Continuous Passive: "Humanitarian organizations are continuously replenishing medical care and supplies."
"Medical care and supplies are being continuously replenished by humanitarian organizations."
9.What do "their" (¶1, flee their homes) and "where" (¶2, shelters, where medical care...) refer to?
"their" = the residents' own homes; "where" = the temporary emergency shelters.
10.How does the writer blend direct and reported speech in the final paragraph?
The direct quote from the rescue coordinator adds authenticity and emotion, while officials' statements are reported indirectly, summarising information efficiently without needing exact wording.

SCENE TWO: THE TENDER SHORE

The setting sun casts a long shadow across an empty wooden pier. ALAN (13), carrying a heavily taped cardboard box, sits on a bench. His older sister, CARA (20), stands a few paces behind him, checking her watch repeatedly.

CARA (Sighing deeply)
Alan, you're not still brooding over that old box, are you? We have to move before the tide climbs.
ALAN (Without looking up)
But where are the real journals?
CARA
I already told you. They took it in turns to load the packing crates yesterday. Some items got misplaced. It happens.
ALAN
No. You're hiding them. You think I can't take care of Grandfather's letters?
CARA (Correcting him sharply)
It is "May I" handle them, Alan. And no, you shouldn't be reading them right now.
ALAN (Echoing mockingly)
"May I" handle them? Er... we are standing on a collapsing pier and you are giving me a grammar lesson?
CARA (Frustrated)
Ugh! Why must you make a mistake out of every decision I try to execute for this family? Look at me. Concentrate! If we do not make tracks to the mainland station immediately, we will get lost in the marshlands after dusk.
ALAN (Standing up, clutching the box tight)
Who spent months saving this house from the bank? Grandfather did. Not you.
CARA (A sharp intake of breath)
Ah! How should I know how to fix everything perfectly? I am doing my absolute best. You think this move is a wonderful holiday for me?
ALAN (Looking out at the water, tone dropping into quiet defeat)
Hmmmm... The horizon looks so small from here. Phew. If I take a deep breath, I can still smell his pipe tobacco on these wooden planks.
CARA (Softening, stepping closer, she places a hand lightly on his shoulder)
Give it a chance, Alan. Just take a deep breath and let's walk. Okay?
Task A · Characterisation & Irony
1.Describe the personality difference between Alan and Cara in their opening exchange.
Alan is emotional and stubborn, fixed on the past and Grandfather's belongings; Cara is practical and anxious, trying to manage the move and keep things moving forward.
2.What dramatic irony does the audience experience regarding the packing of the boxes?
Cara's tone hints she may know more about the missing journals than she admits, while Alan believes she is simply hiding them — the audience senses a gap between what is said and what is really going on.
3.How do the italicised stage directions show a shifting dynamic between the siblings?
Directions move from "sighing," "frustrated," and "a sharp intake of breath" to "softening, stepping closer" — showing Cara move from tension to tenderness by the scene's end.
Task B · Dialogue & Question Analysis
4.What question type is "But where are the real journals?" and what does it reveal about Alan's goal?
An open (Wh-) question. It reveals his immediate goal: locating Grandfather's real journals/letters.
5.What type of question is Alan's ""May I" handle them?" and what tone does it convey?
An echo question — repeating Cara's correction. It conveys mockery and irritation at being corrected mid-crisis.
6.Identify Cara's rhetorical question and explain why she doesn't expect an answer.
"How should I know how to fix everything perfectly?" — it's an outburst of frustration, not a genuine request for information.
Task C · Grammar, Delexical Verbs & Sounds
7.Find where Cara corrects Alan's grammar. Explain the rule difference between the two verbs.
"It is 'May I' handle them" — "can" expresses ability, while "may" is used to request or grant permission.
8.Identify the imperative Cara uses to demand Alan's attention.
"Concentrate!"
9.State the contextual meaning of "took it in turns" and "make tracks."
"took it in turns" = alternated, did it one after another; "make tracks" = leave quickly, hurry away.
10.What emotional transition do "Ugh!" and "Phew" reflect?
"Ugh!" reflects frustration/exasperation; "Phew" reflects relief or a release of built-up tension.

Dramatic Writing — Scripted Dialogue

Task: Write a play-script scene between a strict School Principal and a nervous newly-arrived Student Trainee on their first morning.

SCENE THREE: THE PRINCIPAL'S OFFICE

The room is lined with heavy oak bookshelves. MR. HARRIS (50) stands by the window looking out at the courtyard. ELLEN (19) sits tensely in a chair, twisting a pen between her fingers.

MR. HARRIS (Turning around, raising an eyebrow)
So, Ellen, you're not planning to make a habit of arriving late to your teaching assignments, are you?

ELLEN (Nervously, clearing her throat)
1._____ [speech sound, e.g. Er...] No, Mr. Harris. The train system was delayed. I got completely 2._____ [delexical collocation, e.g. lost on my way] near the eastern junction.

MR. HARRIS
In this academy, we expect our trainee educators to 3._____ [delexical collocation, e.g. take care of] their scheduling commitments. 4._____ [rhetorical question, e.g. How should I expect the students to respect the clock if their instructor does not?]

ELLEN
I understand, sir. 5._____ [permission question, e.g. May I please have my classroom allocation now?]

MR. HARRIS (Walking over to his desk, picking up a heavy file)
Your class assignment? 6._____ [echo question] You haven't even reviewed our core safety protocols yet. 7._____ [imperative, e.g. Concentrate! / Look here!] This manual must be thoroughly memorised before you step in front of the learners.

ELLEN (Softly, looking down)
8._____ [speech sound, e.g. Hmmm...] I see. My instructional design tutor told me I would receive a practical orientation first.

MR. HARRIS
Your tutor? 9._____ [subject question, e.g. Who gave her the authorisation to promise that?] Things are managed differently here. We expect trainees to step up and 10._____ [delexical collocation, e.g. take a decision] under pressure. Now, take this manual and report to Room 4B.

Include at least: 3 speech sounds · 3 delexical collocations (get lost, take care, take a decision) · 1 imperative · 4 question types (tag, rhetorical, permission, echo, subject).
